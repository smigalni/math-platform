# Mathematics content authoring

1. Read the applicable book-specific standards and the relevant common pedagogy, design and quality standards.
2. Inspect neighboring sections to match notation, voice, structure, prerequisite knowledge and difficulty.
3. Confirm the intended curriculum level and what has already been taught. Do not introduce later methods prematurely.
4. Draft clear explanations, worked examples and exercises with a deliberate progression.
5. Ensure every exercise has a mathematically valid solution; check calculations independently.
6. Keep examples distinct from student tasks. Place solutions where the book's established structure expects them.
7. Follow the book's existing Markdown, LaTeX, admonition and navigation conventions. Do not invent formatting.
8. Review the rendered page when formatting or mathematical notation changes.
9. Report assumptions, unresolved content decisions and checks actually performed.

Do not change curriculum scope, chapter architecture or established terminology without flagging the decision for the project owner.
